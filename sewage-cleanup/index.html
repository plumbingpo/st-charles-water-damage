Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. St. Charles Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout St. Charles, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Our Sewage Cleanup Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose St. Charles Restoration:

1

Safety Assessment

First the crew identifies hazards, determines how much of the property the sewage has contaminated and puts containment in place to protect rooms that were not affected.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

Standing sewage is pumped out completely. Contaminated porous materials including carpet, padding, drywall and insulation are removed and properly disposed of as biohazard waste.

3

Cleaning and Disinfecting

EPA-registered antimicrobials are used to disinfect what is left after a thorough cleaning of each surface. Meanwhile, air scrubbers fitted with HEPA filters pull pathogens and other contaminants from the air.

4

Drying & Odor Elimination

Residual moisture is taken out with commercial-grade dryers. Industrial deodorization eliminates sewage odors. Finally, a clearance test verifies that people can safely use the area again.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
