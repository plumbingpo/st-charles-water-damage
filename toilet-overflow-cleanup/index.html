Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in St. Charles

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

Our St. Charles crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. Clean water means extraction and drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the SupplyFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • Septic systems that are full or drain fields that are waterlogged, which send water back toward the house.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Lingering Odor

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Baseboards and door casings sit right at floor level and soak up water from the bottom edge. Most are made of fiberboard, which swells, splits and does not go back to its original shape.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the overflow stayed in the bathroom or reached carpet, closets or a hall outside it.
  • Whether the toilet is upstairs and the ceiling or walls below got wet.
  • The amount of time that passed before the water was extracted and cleanup started.
  • If fixtures such as the toilet or the vanity must come out to reach wet material below.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Identify the Category

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

3

Clean and Disinfect

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Drying and Final Readings

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.
