Kitchen Water Damage: What St. Charles Homeowners Should Know

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in St. Charles, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.

Flooring Over Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Under the Base Cabinets

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Sink Drain Leaks

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

How We Fix It

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
