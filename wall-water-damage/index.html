Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What St. Charles Homeowners Should Know

Drywall acts like a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • A wall that feels cool to the touch compared with others
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb

What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Growth Behind the Paint

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.
